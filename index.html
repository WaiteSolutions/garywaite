It started with problems nobody wanted. Insurance loss consulting — navigating situations people didn't see coming — taught me that every disaster has a structure, and structure can be read. That skill never left. It just changed costumes.

One chapter involved writing for science that later made the news for the wrong reasons — ██████████ — and hot water teaches precision like nothing else. When every sentence can be read by a market and a regulator, you stop writing loose. Ecommerce was the gentle counterweight: an abandoned cart is just a message that failed to land, and nobody ever subpoenaed a product page.

Somewhere in there: viral campaigns I'm not at liberty to over-explain (the numbers live in the dossier), code that ships, research rabbit holes with no bottom, and a mat in a gym in North Texas where the real lessons get taught.

There's also a title on the résumé — talent management — that started as an inside joke and stayed because it's true: I've loved hiring and collaborating with designers across every artistic spectrum and researchers who dive until they hit bedrock. Advocate, position, then get out of their way. The marriage ended; the genetics debate didn't. Certain traits turned out to be dominant — source contested, results not. The next generation already files better bug reports than most of my clients.

The job is technically out of scope — it doesn't appear on invoices, and the real motive is raising kids who grow into good stewards of the gym, their fitness, and themselves. It also happens to be the most honest leadership training money can't buy. Field notes, declassified:

▸

position_before_panic

A kid who panics gets taken down. A kid who moves first, controls the center, and stays off their heels — dictates the match. Campaigns work identically. The brands that win aren't the loudest; they're the ones already in position when the moment shoots.

▸

coach_the_one_in_front_of_you

Twelve kids, twelve operating systems. The drill that works for one confuses another. That's not a kid problem — it's a copy problem wearing a singlet. One message never fits a room. Segment or lose them.

▸

the_loss_review

Nobody improves on the win. We watch the losses — frame by frame, no blame, just pattern. The best analytics meeting of my week happens in a folding chair after a kid loses 4–2 and asks what he missed.

▸

pressure_with_purpose

Pressure without direction is just noise — and kids can smell noise. Same with audiences. Every push you put on a reader has to earn the right to move them somewhere they'll thank you for.

▸

get_up_one_more

The only stat that matters in youth sports isn't on the scoreboard. It's whether they come back next season. Retention beats conquest. It always has. On the mat, in email, in life.
